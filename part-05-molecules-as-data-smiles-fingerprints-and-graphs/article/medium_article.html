Why a Molecule Needs a Representation

Two different strings can describe the same molecule, and two different molecules can share a formula. A prediction model reads numbers while a chemist draws pictures, so every project that learns from molecules needs a step in the middle. That step is a representation, and each one keeps some information and drops other information.

Part 1: Recover the Molecular Graph

A Molecule as a Map of Towns and Roads

Three towns joined by two roads in a line form a graph, where towns are nodes and roads are edges. Name the towns carbon, carbon and oxygen, with single-bond roads, and the map is ethanol. Rename the last town carbon, and the roads look the same while the molecule changes. So a graph holds the pattern of roads and the label on every town and road.

Counting Hydrogens, Formula and Mass

Every atom has hands, called valence: carbon 4, nitrogen 3, oxygen 2, hydrogen 1, and in a stable molecule every hand holds something. In ethanol, the first carbon has three hands left (3 hydrogens), the second carbon two (2) and the oxygen one (1), so a drawing can leave the six hydrogens out.

Ethanol is C2H6O, and with carbon at 12.011, hydrogen at 1.008 and oxygen at 15.999 grams per mole, we get 24.022 + 6.048 + 15.999 = 46.069, the number RDKit reports.

Same Atoms, Different Connections

The words listen and silent share their letters, and ethanol and dimethyl ether (C-O-C) share C2H6O and a mass of 46.069. Hydrogen-bond donors separate them: ethanol has one, and dimethyl ether has zero.

Tables, Adjacency and Renumbering

An adjacency matrix is the table at the back of a road atlas. For ethanol’s atoms 0, 1 and 2, the rows read 010, 101 and 010, and the row sums 1, 2 and 1 count each atom’s neighbors. The function graph_data exports this matrix with atom and bond tables.

Renumbering from the oxygen end gives the same matrix with the labels moved, like friends turning around in a line and renumbering their shirts. To compare two graphs we look for a mapping that matches every label and bond: one exists for ethanol and its reversed numbering, and none exists between ethanol and dimethyl ether.

Mirror Partners and the InChIKey

Left and right gloves have the same parts and connections, and each fits one hand. Lactic acid has a central carbon with four different groups, which gives two mirror partners with the same formula C3H6O3, the same mass of 90.078 and the same connections. This handedness is called chirality.

An InChIKey is a passport number for a molecule, with 27 characters in three blocks: connections, finer details such as stereochemistry, and protonation. The lactic acid partners share the first block, JVTAAEKCZFNVCJ, and their second blocks start REOHCLBH and UWTATZPH. We join records only when the full keys agree, because a name can point to several structures.

Part 2: Read and Check Molecular Strings

SMILES is a line notation whose letters and symbols describe atoms, bonds, branches and rings (Weininger, 1988). We check strings with RDKit, an open-source chemistry toolkit.

SMILES as Walking Directions

Directions from the station to the bakery to the park redraw a route, and reading a SMILES string from left to right redraws the molecule. Different directions can reach the same map: CCO and OCC are two spellings of ethanol, so we compare molecules by rebuilding the graph.

The Alphabet, Branches and Rings

A capital letter is an atom, neighbors share a single bond, an equals sign is a double bond, parentheses open a branch, a digit closes a ring like a rubber band stretched to a remembered atom, a dot separates pieces, brackets hold charges and an at sign records handedness.

Acetic acid, CC(=O)O, has four atoms and three bonds, and the hands rule gives hydrogen counts 3, 0, 0 and 1, so C2H4O2 and 60.052 grams per mole. Benzene, c1ccccc1, has six atoms, and the closing digit adds a sixth bond. With one hydrogen on each carbon, the formula is C6H6 and the mass is 6 × 12.011 + 6 × 1.008 = 78.114.

A Dot, as a Bag Holding Two Things

A dot is a bag holding a key and a coin. Sodium acetate, CC(=O)[O-].[Na+], holds an acetate piece (four atoms, charge minus one) and a sodium piece (one atom, charge plus one): five atoms, two components, total charge zero. RDKit parses it, but our matrix keeps one connected molecule per row, so the row gets the status excluded_multi_component with a written reason. Salts are valid chemistry, and silently keeping the largest fragment would change what we represented.

Two Layers of Checking

A sentence can break its grammar, or read smoothly and mean something odd, like “the stone drank the idea”. Molecules get both checks. Parsing is the grammar check: C1CC leaves a ring open and fails with a syntax error. Sanitizing is the chemistry check: CO(C)C parses, but its oxygen touches three carbons and has only two hands. An empty string and a missing value are rejected rows too.

molecule = Chem.MolFromSmiles(value, sanitize=False)   # step 1: syntax only
problem = Chem.SanitizeMol(molecule, catchErrors=True)  # step 2: chemistry

Canonical SMILES and the At Sign

Canonicalization gives every molecule one catalog number: the program rebuilds the graph and writes it with one fixed rule. Our three ethanol spellings collapse into CCO and one InChIKey, with the raw text kept beside them.

The at sign encodes handedness: looking from the first neighbor toward the central atom, the remaining neighbors run counterclockwise for one at sign and clockwise for two. Lactic acid C[C@H](O)C(=O)O and its mirror partner C[C@@H](O)C(=O)O differ in that symbol only, and removing it turns both into CC(O)C(=O)O, which loses information.

SELFIES and SAFE, an Overview

SELFIES (Krenn et al., 2020) is a spell checker for molecules, because strings in its supported language decode to valid graphs. SAFE (Noutahi et al., 2024) organizes a molecule as connected fragments, like chapters a model can edit. Both stay at overview level, because our code uses SMILES with RDKit.

Part 3: Build and Compare Fingerprints

A Fingerprint as a Checklist

Imagine a long checklist: is there an oxygen, is there a ring? We tick a box for every yes, and every molecule uses the same checklist in the same order, so the row of ones and zeros becomes a vector. Morgan fingerprints grow their questions from the molecules, and MACCS keys use a fixed list.

Descriptors and Polar Surface Area

A descriptor summarizes one aspect of structure as a number, and the lab exports seven, such as average mass and topological polar surface area (TPSA). TPSA adds a fixed piece for each polar atom, in square angstroms: 20.23 for a hydroxyl oxygen, 9.23 for an ether oxygen and 17.07 for a carbonyl oxygen. Acetic acid gives 17.07 + 20.23 = 37.30, and lactic acid 20.23 + 17.07 + 20.23 = 57.53, matching the saved features.csv.

Neighborhoods that Grow

At radius 0 you know only your own house, at radius 1 the houses next door, and at radius 2 the neighbors of your neighbors. A Morgan fingerprint describes every atom as a growing circle like this. Ethanol has three circles at radius 0 and three at radius 1, while radius 2 adds nothing new, so it has six environments. Our fingerprint uses radius 2, related to the extended-connectivity fingerprints of Rogers and Hahn (2010).

Hashing and Folding, with a Coat Check

Each environment gets a large whole-number identifier by hashing. Folding divides it by 2048 and keeps the remainder as the bit position, like a coat check with a fixed number of hooks: with eight hooks, tickets 17 and 25 both leave remainder 1 and share hook 1. For ethanol’s oxygen, 864,662,311 = 422,198 × 2048 + 807, so bit 807 switches on, and the other five environments land on bits 80, 222, 294, 1057 and 1410.

Benzene shows symmetry: its six identical carbons give 18 environments that use only three identifiers, so three bits switch on with counts 6, 6 and 6. Equal neighborhoods get equal identifiers, so the hydroxyl oxygens of ethanol and acetic acid both land on bit 807.

"folded_bit": identifier % fp_size,   # the coat-check rule in one line

Collisions, Bits and Counts

A collision means two different features share one bit, which differs from repetition, where one feature occurs several times. Acetic acid has eight identifiers on seven bits, because its hydroxyl oxygen and acid carbon both leave remainder 807. Erlotinib has 48 identifiers on 47 bits, because two identifiers both land on bit 1452. The function collision_audit finds these cases.

A bit says a feature is present, and a count says how often. Dimethyl ether has two identical methyl groups, so bits 1057 and 841 each count twice, and bits 695 and 975 once: four bits on and 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 6 occurrences. Bits suit a quick overlap, and counts suit questions about repetition.

Tanimoto, as Two Shopping Lists

Ana’s list holds bread, milk and eggs, and Ben’s holds bread, eggs and rice. They share two items out of four different items in total (3 + 3 - 2), so the overlap is 2 / 4 = 0.5. That is the Tanimoto coefficient: shared on-bits divided by the positions on in either fingerprint.

Now the real fingerprints. Ethanol has six bits on, and dimethyl ether has four. They share only bit 1057, and the union holds 6 + 4 - 1 = 9 bits, so the Tanimoto similarity is 1/9, about 0.111. The molecules share a formula and a mass, yet their local neighborhoods differ almost everywhere. A score describes two encodings under recorded settings.

Chirality and MACCS Keys

With chirality included, each lactic acid partner switches on 11 bits and nine are shared, so the Tanimoto is 9 / (11 + 11 - 9) = 0.692. With chirality off, the two fingerprints are identical and the Tanimoto is 1. The setting decides which differences the fingerprint can see, so we record it.

MACCS keys are a fixed questionnaire written in advance. RDKit stores 167 positions for 166 keys, because position 0 is a placeholder, and ethanol has nine positions on. A MACCS position asks the same question for every molecule, while a folded Morgan bit can hold several features.

Part 4: Run an Auditable Representation Lab

We use 20 deliberately chosen rows: equivalent strings, distinct molecules, a salt, incomplete stereochemistry and explicit failures. Every exclusion keeps its reason.

The Map, the Checks and the Run

All of the code lives in molecular_representation_lab.py: parse_input reads each string in two layers, describe returns the canonical string, InChIKey and descriptors, generator and fingerprint_bundle build the Morgan fingerprint, collision_audit and similarity check and compare it, and build_payload assigns every row a status and writes the tables.

The program first validates the saved inputs from earlier lessons: exact compound keys and SHA-256 file hashes, and a deliberately changed copy was refused. The fingerprint settings (radius 2, 2048 bits, chirality on) and the RDKit version are saved in representation_settings.json. To run it, install requirements.txt, then run worked_examples.py and molecular_representation_lab.py. Our saved results used Python 3.12 with RDKit 2026.09.2.

The Audit, as a Laundry Sort

Sorting laundry into baskets keeps the total number of items the same, and our audit sorts the 20 rows into five baskets: 8 accepted_primary, 5 alias, 1 excluded_multi_component, 2 excluded_unspecified_stereo and 4 rejected. The running sum 8, 13, 14, 16, 20 checks the program.

The 8 primary identities are ethanol, dimethyl ether, benzene, the two lactic acid partners, acetic acid, erlotinib and the exact MRTX1133 component. The 5 aliases, such as OCC and C(O)C, must recover their parent’s canonical string and key, or the run stops. The excluded rows are the salt, an unspecified-stereo lactic acid and a name-search record for MRTX1133, and the 4 rejected rows are C1CC, CO(C)C, an empty string and a missing value. Equivalent strings count as one compound, which keeps training and test examples independent.

A Join, as a Library Card

Erlotinib’s measurement from lesson four stays a separate record: 2.6 nanomolar, activity 13852013, assay CHEMBL3095060, endpoint IC50. The compound key plays the part of a library card number, and a join on the key attaches the measurement to that one molecule. The other teaching inputs keep blank activity cells, and missing values stay missing.

Tests and Their Limits

The 22 tests check that equivalent strings agree, different connectivity stays distinct, reordering preserves the graph, stereochemistry survives, invalid inputs keep their failures, and the collision and similarity arithmetic matches. They establish the tested behavior, while activity and clinical effect need their own evidence.

Wrap-Up

A molecule becomes data through explicit representation rules. SMILES writes a graph as a string, descriptors summarize selected properties, and fingerprints compress selected environments into numerical patterns, with settings and possible information loss. Our run exposes aliases, stereochemical differences and a genuine folding collision rather than hiding them.
