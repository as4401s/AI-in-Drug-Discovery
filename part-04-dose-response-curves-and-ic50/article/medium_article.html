Why a Number Needs Context

A table says IC50 = 12 in one row and 3 in another. Which compound is more potent? That depends on the unit, the assay (enzyme or living cell), what “half” means for that curve, and how many wells stand behind each number. A potency value is a small package of evidence. We use a synthetic dataset from our own program, so we know the truth, and real erlotinib records from ChEMBL (release 37). Background reading: Beck et al. (2017) and Hafner et al. (2016).

Part 1: Make a Response Measurement

An Enzyme Assay, with a Bakery Line

Picture a bakery line where workers (the enzymes) turn dough into rolls. A clear aisle gives 100 rolls a minute, and blockers in the aisle slow it to 60. The output fell by 40 percent, which we call 40 percent inhibition. A compound that blocks an enzyme plays the role of the blockers.

Concentration, Units and Dilution

Concentration is the amount of compound in each litre, and units step by thousands: 0.25 micromolar is 250 nanomolar. So 10 nanomolar is 100 times smaller than 1 micromolar, even though 10 is larger than 1. We always write the unit and convert before comparing. A dilution series steps down by one factor, like glasses of squash that are each ten times weaker. Ours uses about threefold steps: 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10, 30, 100, 300 and 1,000 nanomolar.

Controls and Normalization

A race needs a start and a finish line. The vehicle control (everything except the compound) reads about 1,000 units and marks 0 percent inhibition. The fully inhibited reference reads about 100 units and marks 100 percent. Normalization subtracts the test signal from the vehicle signal, divides by the distance between the lines (900) and multiplies by 100. A reading of 820 gives 180, then 0.2, then 20 percent. We keep values outside 0 to 100, such as minus 4.4, because they carry information about noise.

def normalize(signal, vehicle, inhibited):
    # values outside 0-100 stay visible: clipping would hide noise
    return 100 * (vehicle - np.asarray(signal, dtype=float)) / (vehicle - inhibited)

Replicates, with Cake Photos and Cakes

Three photos of one cake agree, and three separate cakes show how much baking varies. Three wells reading 48, 52 and 50 have a spread (standard deviation) of 2. Three independent days reading 50, 38 and 61 have a spread of about 11.5. Our practice plate has technical wells only, so it describes pipetting and detection noise.

Part 2: Read the Curve

The Log Axis and the IC50

At 1, 10 and 100 nanomolar, inhibition is about 9, 50 and 91 percent. A linear axis crowds these points at the far left, and a log ruler gives every tenfold step the same room, because the logarithms of 1, 10, 100 and 1,000 are 0, 1, 2 and 3. The IC50 is where the response reaches halfway between its plateaus, like the moment a stadium is half full. A stadium with another capacity has another halfway mark, so plateaus and slope need their own knobs.

Four Parameters and the Hill Formula

A response model is a mixing desk with four knobs: bottom, top, midpoint and slope. The formula is

response = bottom + (top - bottom) / (1 + (midpoint / concentration) ^ slope)

With bottom 0, top 100, midpoint 10 nanomolar and slope 2, the response at 20 nanomolar is 10 / 20 = 0.5; squared 0.25; plus 1 gives 1.25; 1 / 1.25 = 0.8; times 100 gives 80 percent.

def hill(concentration_nm, midpoint_nm, slope, bottom=0.0, top=100.0):
    x = np.asarray(concentration_nm, dtype=float)
    return bottom + (top - bottom) / (1 + (midpoint_nm / np.maximum(x, 1e-300)) ** slope)

Slope, with a Dimmer and a Switch

A gentle slope is a dimmer, and a steep one is a switch, yet both reach halfway brightness at the same dial position. With a midpoint of 10 nanomolar, slopes 1, 2 and 4 give 33.33, 20 and 5.88 percent at 5 nanomolar, and 66.67, 80 and 94.12 at 20 nanomolar. The midpoint alone leaves the sharpness open.

Relative and Absolute 50 Percent

A class whose best mark is 40 has a halfway point of 20 marks, and the 50-mark line stays out of reach. A compound that tops out at 40 percent inhibition behaves the same way: its relative midpoint gives 20 percent. A curve with bottom 10, top 70 and midpoint 100 nanomolar has a midpoint response of 40 percent, and its absolute 50 percent crossing sits at 200 nanomolar. So “IC50” can mean two things, and reports should say which.

Potency, Efficacy and Affinity

Two cars: A starts with a light touch and has a modest top speed, and B needs a firmer push and goes much faster. Compound A has an IC50 of 5 nanomolar and a maximum of 40 percent, and compound B has 50 nanomolar and 95 percent. At 100 nanomolar, A gives 38.1 and B gives 63.33. Potency and efficacy are different questions, and selectivity, exposure and toxicity each need their own evidence.

Affinity is a separate property. For a simple competitive inhibitor, the Cheng-Prusoff relation (Cheng and Prusoff, 1973) says IC50 = Ki x (1 + S / Km). With a Ki of 10 nanomolar, the IC50 is 20 when S equals Km, 50 at four times Km, and 100 at nine times Km. The same grip gives three IC50 values, so we keep the substrate level and method next to every value.

pIC50, with a pH-Style Scale

Write the IC50 in molar, take the base-ten logarithm and flip the sign. 1 micromolar gives 6, 100 nanomolar gives 7, 10 nanomolar gives 8, and 3 nanomolar gives 8.52. Each unit up means a tenfold lower concentration, so a higher pIC50 means a more potent compound under a matching definition.

Part 3: Fit and Check in Python

The program builds a practice plate with a known truth, fits the four-parameter curve, measures the error, redraws wells to see how far the answer moves, and refuses to report a number when the data stop short of 50 percent. The code lives in dose_response_lab.py (normalize, hill, pic50, synthetic_data, fit_hill, bootstrap).

The Practice Plate

The truth is a midpoint of 10 nanomolar, a slope of 1 and plateaus at 0 and 100. The program converts the true inhibition at nine concentrations to signals and adds a random wiggle of about 12 units, with a seed so every learner gets the same numbers. Three wells per concentration give 27 test wells, normalized and averaged.

The Error, with Darts

Fitting is throwing darts, where each throw is a pair of knob settings. With observed values 12, 49 and 93 and a candidate predicting 9, 50 and 91, the residuals are +3, -1 and +2. Squares are 9, 1 and 4, their average is 4.67, and the square root is 2.16, the root mean squared error (RMSE). A flat line at 50 gives 33.14.

Searching for the Best Midpoint

Hiking a foggy hillside, we test a few spots, then explore around the lowest. With a slope of 1, midpoints of 1, 3, 10, 30 and 100 nanomolar give errors of 32.85, 17.94, 2.15, 17.43 and 34.46. The program runs a 41 by 41 grid of midpoints and slopes, keeps the best cell, shrinks the box around it and repeats eight times. This teaching optimizer keeps every step readable, and production work uses validated regression software. On our plate it recovered a midpoint of 9.78 nanomolar, a slope of 1.00, a pIC50 of 8.01 and an RMSE of 0.45, against 39.23 for a constant.

Residual Plots

Residuals of +3, -1, +2, -2 and +1 behave like noise. Residuals of +6, +4, +1, -3 and -7 form a smooth run, which points to a wrong shape such as a missing plateau. One summary error can hide that, so we inspect residuals against concentration.

Bootstrap, with Names in a Hat

Draw slips from a hat holding three wells (48, 52 and 50), putting each back. Draws of 48, 48, 52 give a mean of 49.33, and 52, 50, 50 give 50.67. The means wander around 50 and show how much wells could shift the mean. The program redraws wells at all nine concentrations and refits 400 times. The middle 95 percent of fitted midpoints runs from 9.32 to 10.27 nanomolar. This interval reflects technical wells only, and new days and preparations add uncertainty it leaves out.

Bracketing and Refusal

A thirty-centimetre ruler against a longer table gives the honest report “greater than 30 centimetres”. Means of 5, 14, 27, 35 and 38 percent top out at 38, so 50 percent lies beyond the highest concentration, and fit_hill returns refused with the direction of the bound. Means of 22, 41 and 63 bracket 50, so a crossing can be estimated. Further checks use disclosed teaching thresholds, such as an error above 5 percentage points.

Why Growth Matters, with Two Cell Lines

A fast line grows from 1,000 to 4,000 cells untreated, and a slow line from 1,000 to 2,000. A drug that halves the growth rate leaves 2,000 fast cells (50 percent viability) and 1,414 slow cells (70.7 percent). The same drug gives two percentages. Growth rate inhibition metrics (Hafner et al., 2016) give 0.414 for both, so we record growth and timing context beside every potency value.

Part 4: Curate Potency Data We Can Trust

Curation, with a Library Return Desk

Every returned book is checked before it goes on a shelf, and every activity record passes a desk that checks compound, target, endpoint, number, unit and relation. Of six invented rows, an equals 12 nanomolar row on the enzyme goes on the shelf, a greater than 10,000 nanomolar row waits as a bound, a 3 micromolar row becomes 3,000 nanomolar, a cell-assay row goes to the cell shelf, and a possible duplicate is excluded with the flag as its reason. The function classify records every reason.

The Real Records

The script checks the Part 3 manifest, the EGFR accession and the exact identity of erlotinib, and stops if anything differs. We download all 209 IC50 records for erlotinib on human EGFR and select 12 teaching cases to exercise the checks. High target-assignment confidence describes the target mapping, and experimental quality needs its own evidence.

Bounds and Assay Groups

A bathroom scale that stops at 150 kilograms shows 150 for a heavy suitcase, so the weight is 150 or more. A greater than 10,000 nanomolar IC50 is such a reading: in molar it is greater than 10 to the minus 5, and the pIC50 is less than 5. Replacing the bound with 5 would turn a range into a precise value, so bounds stay in their own group. A run and a flight share a number and a unit, and an enzyme value of 50 nanomolar and a cell value of 200 would average to 125, a mixture of two experiments. So each assay group stays separate.

What the Curation Kept

Of the 12 selected cases, one is retained: activity 13852013, an IC50 of 2.6 nanomolar from an assay described as wild-type EGFR, with an incompletely specified construct and substrate concentration. The other 11 keep their reasons: 2 censored, 2 cell-based, 2 possible duplicates, 2 unknown variant, 1 missing value, 1 T790M mutant and 1 low target confidence. Our pIC50 of 8.585027 agrees with the ChEMBL value of 8.59. If we mix targets, variants or conditions casually, a computer can learn the mixture instead of the biology.

Check Your Understanding

Try these first, then compare with the answers.

  1. Convert 100 nanomolar to molar and calculate its pIC50.
  2. A curve rises from 0 to 40 percent inhibition with a relative midpoint of 10 nanomolar. What is the inhibition at that midpoint, and where is the absolute 50 percent crossing?

Answers: (1) 100 nanomolar is 10 to the minus 7 molar, so the pIC50 is 7. (2) The response at the midpoint is 20 percent, and the curve stays below 50, so there is no absolute crossing and the IC50 is best reported as a bound.

Wrap-Up

An IC50 summarizes a specified halfway response under specified assay and fitting conditions. It becomes useful when the response definition, plateaus, tested range, units, uncertainty and source stay attached. Our Python fit recovered about 9.78 nanomolar from a reproducible synthetic example, and our curation kept real records together with the reasons to keep assays apart. In the next lesson, we ask how a molecule becomes data that a computer can use. Hope this article helps all those who want to read a potency number carefully before they use it as a label.
